I went to Dayara Bugyal from 11th to 15th November, this time with friends. It was a different kind of trek from the ones I've done on my own or with family, and the difference had very little to do with the mountain.

The mountain was lovely. November meant cold air, and on the side that didn't get much sun there was still a bit of snow lying around, which I hadn't expected to see at all. But the thing I'll remember is the pace.

I walk fast when I trek. It's the one place where I can switch my head off and just move. On this trek I couldn't. Some of the group were slower, and one of my friends wasn't feeling well on the climb. They were tired, struggling, and honestly close to wanting to stop. So the real work of the trek became staying with them: slowing down, waiting, talking them through the next stretch, and pulling them along until we all reached the summit together.

It was a real test of patience, and I wasn't always graceful about it. Moving at someone else's speed is harder than it sounds when you're used to your own. But I think it was good for me. At work, and in most of life, I'm used to the question being how fast I can get somewhere. Here, the only question that mattered was whether we all got there.

We did. Seeing my friend finish a climb they'd half-decided they couldn't, and standing at the top with everyone, was worth far more than the time I "lost" on the way up.

Dayara Bugyal is now on my list next to Brahmatal, Chandrashila, Kedarkantha and Kheerganga. It's the one where I learned the most about walking with other people.
