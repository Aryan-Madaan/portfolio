You have two unmarked jugs, one that holds exactly **5 gallons** and one that holds exactly **3 gallons**, plus an unlimited water supply and a drain. You can fill a jug completely, empty a jug completely, or pour one into the other until the source is empty or the target is full. There's no other way to measure.

**How do you end up with exactly 4 gallons in the 5-gallon jug?**

<Spoiler>

Track the contents as (5-gallon jug, 3-gallon jug):

| Step | Action | State |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fill the 5-gallon jug | (5, 0) |
| 2 | Pour into the 3-gallon jug until full | (2, 3) |
| 3 | Empty the 3-gallon jug | (2, 0) |
| 4 | Pour the remaining 2 gallons into the 3-gallon jug | (0, 2) |
| 5 | Fill the 5-gallon jug again | (5, 2) |
| 6 | Pour into the 3-gallon jug until full (it only has room for 1 more) | (4, 1) |

The 5-gallon jug now holds exactly **4 gallons**.

There's a second route: fill the 3-gallon jug and pour it into the 5, fill the 3 again and top up the 5 (leaving 1 in the 3), empty the 5, pour that 1 gallon across, fill the 3 once more and pour it in to get 1 + 3 = 4. It takes more steps, which is why the first route is the one to remember.

It works because 3 and 5 share no common factor, so differences of those two sizes can produce every whole number up to 5. With jugs of 4 and 6 you could never measure an odd amount.

</Spoiler>
